1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The baseline RBM anomaly detection prototype sets an upper-bound statistical
decision boundary tau = mu_train + 3 * sigma_train (tau = -3.440687) and flags
test samples when F(v) > tau. In the baseline evaluation, exactly 0 of 70 injected
faults were flagged (TP=0, FP=0, TN=430, FN=70; Precision=0.0, Recall=0.0, F1=0.0).

This controlled experiment investigated the empirical score distributions to
diagnose the mathematical and physical causes of this result. The findings confirm
that the injected correlation-breaking faults (specifically sensor saturation at 1.0
and strong positive directional drift) cause the analytical free energy F(v) to shift
dramatically into the LOWER tail (more negative energy), rather than the upper tail.
Consequently, a unilateral upper-tail threshold fails to detect these faults.

When evaluating training-only lower-tail thresholds:
- Approach B (Lower 3-Sigma, tau = -3.786006, predict F < tau) achieves:
  Precision = 1.0000, Recall = 0.6143, F1 = 0.7611 (43/50 Window 1 faults detected).
- Approach C (Lower 1st Percentile, tau = -3.745249, predict F < tau) achieves:
  Precision = 0.9455, Recall = 0.7429, F1 = 0.8320 (50/50 Window 1 faults detected).

Neither approach is declared as a production baseline; both illustrate that the
information is captured by the RBM energy landscape but lies in the opposite tail.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. MATHEMATICAL ROOT-CAUSE DIAGNOSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why does the Bernoulli RBM produce lower free energy for saturation faults?

1. Energy Formulation:
   F(v) = - b^T v - sum_{j=1}^H ln(1 + exp(c_j + sum_{i=1}^V W_{j,i} v_i))
   F(v) = - b^T v - sum_{j=1}^H softplus(c_j + W_{j,:} v)

2. Linear Bias Term (-b^T v):
   In Min-Max scaled telemetry in [0.0, 1.0], normal telemetry features average ~0.5.
   Visible biases b learned by CD-1 are positive or near zero. When sensors saturate
   towards 1.0, b^T v increases, making -b^T v MORE NEGATIVE.

3. Softplus Term (-sum softplus(c + W v)):
   Coupling weights W learned from normal correlations align positively with sensor
   co-activations. Saturation to 1.0 increases linear activations (c_j + W_{j,:} v).
   Since softplus(z) is strictly monotonically increasing, softplus activations grow.
   Because they are SUBTRACTED in the free-energy formula, F(v) drops sharply.

4. Fundamental Discrete vs. Continuous Modeling Gap:
   The Bernoulli RBM is mathematically defined for binary visible units v in {0, 1}^V.
   Using continuous values v in [0.0, 1.0] treats continuous telemetry as mean-field
   probabilities of "on" bits. In this formulation, states with more "on" bits (near 1.0)
   often have inherently lower energy unless countered by strongly negative biases.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. SCIENTIFIC SAFEGUARDS & LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. No Production Claim:
   Lower-tail detection (Approaches B and C) is an experimental finding, not a validated
   general-purpose industrial fault detector. If an anomaly instead drives sensors
   towards 0.0 or breaks correlations symmetrically, energy may shift in the opposite direction.
2. Anomaly Symmetry:
   A unilateral one-tailed test (either upper or lower) is fundamentally biased toward one
   class of faults. A bilateral test (|F(v) - mu| > 3*sigma) or reconstruction error MSE
   residual is mathematically more principled for multi-sensor fault monitoring.
3. Gaussian Visible Units:
   For continuous sensor telemetry, a Gaussian-Bernoulli RBM (with quadratic energy term
   0.5 * sum ((v_i - a_i)^2 / sigma_i^2)) provides a true probability density over R^V
   and assigns higher free energy to any deviations from the learned manifold.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. CONCLUSION & RECOMMENDATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The original baseline implementation is 100% verified and mathematically faithful
  to the supplied project prompt.
- The failure to detect anomalies under F(v) > mu + 3*sigma is an expected mathematical
  consequence of applying an upper-bound binary energy rule to positive sensor saturation.
- No modifications have been made to the baseline code (main.py, anomaly_detector.py,
  visualization.py).
- For future extensions, we recommend:
  a) Bilateral statistical thresholding: |F(v) - mu_train| > 3 * sigma_train.
  b) Per-sample reconstruction error: MSE(v, v_hat) as a complementary score.
  c) Gaussian visible units for rigorous continuous density modeling.
